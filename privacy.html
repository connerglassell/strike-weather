Strike Weather shows you the weather. It has no accounts, no ads and no analytics, and the developer runs no server that receives your data.

What the app uses

Your location. With your permission, the app uses your location while it is open to get the forecast for where you are. Your coordinates, rounded to about 10 metres, are sent to the weather service you have chosen in Settings (Apple Weather by default, or the National Weather Service, Open-Meteo, MET Norway, Tomorrow.io or OpenWeather) to fetch the forecast. The location is not stored.

Rain alerts (optional). If you turn on Rain alerts, the app remembers your last position, rounded to about a mile, on your iPhone only, so it can check for rain when the app is closed. It is deleted when you turn Rain alerts off, and it is never sent to iCloud.

Places you search for. What you type in Search is sent to Apple Maps to complete place names. The app keeps no search history.

Radar and maps. Radar, lightning, satellite and warning layers come from NOAA, which sees the map area you are viewing. Tapping the map with Warnings on sends that point to the National Weather Service. Maps and place names come from Apple Maps.

Time Machine and rain heads-ups. The place's coordinates and the date you pick are sent to Open-Meteo for the Time Machine. The rain heads-up banner and Rain alerts also use Open-Meteo's short-range rain forecast (and Apple's, when Apple Weather is your source).

Your own API keys (optional). If you choose Tomorrow.io or OpenWeather, you paste your own free API key. It is kept in your iPhone's Keychain (this device only) and is sent only to that service.

Settings sync. Your preferences (temperature unit, weather source, map layers, and Rain alerts on or off and their timing) sync between your devices through your private iCloud account. Your location, searches and API keys are never synced.

What the app does not do

It does not track you, build a profile, show ads, use analytics or crash-reporting services, or share your data with anyone other than the weather services above, which receive only what is described here. Each of those services has its own privacy policy.

Children

The app is suitable for all ages and collects no personal information about anyone.
