Strike Weather shows you the weather. It has no accounts, no ads and no analytics, and the developer runs no server that receives your data.

What the app uses

Your location. With your permission, the app uses your location while it is open to get the forecast for where you are. Your coordinates, rounded to about 10 metres, are sent to the weather service you have chosen in Settings (Apple Weather by default, or the National Weather Service, Open-Meteo, MET Norway, Tomorrow.io, OpenWeather or WeatherAPI.com) to fetch the forecast. The location is not stored.

Rain alerts (optional). If you turn on Rain alerts, the app remembers your last position, rounded to about a mile, on your iPhone only, so it can check for rain when the app is closed. It is deleted when you turn Rain alerts off, and it is never sent to iCloud.

The rain and snow Live Activity (optional, on by default). While rain or snow is happening or due within the hour where you are, the app can show a Live Activity on your Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. It keeps a position rounded to about a mile on your iPhone while the activity is active, and there is no push server: it only updates while the app itself is able to run, and it never leaves your device. The on-or-off preference syncs like your other settings; the position does not.

Places you search for. What you type in Search is sent to Apple Maps to complete place names. Recent searches are kept only while Search is open and are never stored. If you save a place as a Favorite, its name and coordinates are kept so it appears next time — and, like your other preferences, that syncs between your devices through your private iCloud account.

Radar and maps. Local radar decodes NEXRAD data from NOAA's Open Data (NODD) on AWS on your device, for the site nearest you or one you pick by hand; that request tells NOAA/AWS which radar site and product you're asking for. The wider radar map instead uses NOAA's MRMS mosaic (via nowCOAST) for the US and the Americas, Environment and Climate Change Canada for Canada, or the Deutscher Wetterdienst for Germany, whichever covers the map area you're viewing — that service sees the map area, not who you are. The Day and Week forecast ranges work the same way, using NOAA's HRRR model (also NODD on AWS) for the next day in the US, and DWD ICON or ECMWF IFS open data, fetched through Open-Meteo, elsewhere and for the week ahead. Lightning, satellite, storm-cell tracking, tropical-storm and warning layers come from NOAA (nowCOAST and the National Weather Service); the Wildfires layer comes from NASA FIRMS; the Wind layer comes from Open-Meteo (NOAA's GFS model); the Smoke layer comes from NOAA's HRRR-Smoke model, also on AWS. Each of these sees only the map area or radar site involved, never your identity. Tapping the map with Warnings on sends that point to the National Weather Service. Maps and place names come from Apple Maps.

Time Machine and rain heads-ups. The place's coordinates and the date you pick are sent to Open-Meteo for the Time Machine. The rain heads-up banner and Rain alerts also use Open-Meteo's short-range rain forecast (and Apple's, when Apple Weather is your source).

Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets (optional). If you add a Strike Weather widget and allow it to use your location, the widget asks iOS for your location each time it refreshes and handles it exactly as the app does: your coordinates, rounded the same way, go to the weather service you've chosen (and to Open-Meteo for the short-range rain forecast), and nothing is stored. The radar widget instead fetches the same local or mosaic radar image described above, for the area around your location. So it can show what you've chosen, a widget reads your temperature unit, weather source and any Tomorrow.io, OpenWeather or WeatherAPI.com key you've added — kept on your iPhone only, through a shared App Group and Keychain group that your location is never placed in.

Your own API keys (optional). If you choose Tomorrow.io, OpenWeather or WeatherAPI.com, you paste your own free API key. It is kept in your iPhone's Keychain (this device only) and is sent only to that service.

Settings sync. Your preferences — temperature unit, weather source, map layers and your favorite layers, radar product and site, the rain and snow Live Activity on-off, Rain alerts on or off and their timing, your saved Favorite places, and which Home Screen sections you show and their order — sync between your devices through your private iCloud account. Your location, recent searches and API keys are never synced.

What the app does not do

It does not track you, build a profile, show ads, use analytics or crash-reporting services, or share your data with anyone other than the weather and radar services above, which receive only what is described here. Each of those services has its own privacy policy.

Children

The app is suitable for all ages and collects no personal information about anyone.
